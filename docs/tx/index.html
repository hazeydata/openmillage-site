Texas property tax rates by county, 2021 to 2025

2025 is preliminary (Texas Comptroller Working version). A Working or Final label on the source does not clear this flag.

Between 2021 and 2025, 194 of Texas’s 254 counties lowered their county total property tax rate, 48 raised it and 11 kept it the same. One county has no reported 2025 total rate, so it is left out of that count. Rates below are per $100 of taxable value, straight from Texas Comptroller files.

About the unit. Every rate here is dollars per $100 of taxable value, so 0.5000 means 50 cents per $100. Texas Tax Code states that unit: “dollars per $100 of taxable value” (Tex. Tax Code §26.04(c)), “Total tax rate (per $100 of value)” (§26.062(d)(1)), and for non-ISD bodies “per $100 valuation” (§26.07). Unit basis: statutory_per_100.

School districts and special districts: in the data, not on these pages yet. This page shows county and city rates only. School-district and special-district rates are in the pinned Texas data files but are not listed here yet.

In numbers: 48 counties raised their rate and 11 kept it the same. Biggest rises per $100: Jeff Davis County (+58.7 cents), Willacy County (+56.3 cents) and Baylor County (+38.0 cents).

In numbers: 194 counties lowered their rate. Biggest drops per $100: Cochran County (−26.1 cents), Bee County (−25.2 cents) and Hopkins County (−24.8 cents).
